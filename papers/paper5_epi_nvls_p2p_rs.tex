\title{Zero-HBM-Traffic Collective-GEMM Fusion on Multi-Tray Blackwell NVL72:\\
A Two-Regime Architecture Unifying \texttt{Epi-NVLS} and Bit-Exact \texttt{Epi-P2P-RS}}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Rishi Vinodkumar}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Tensor-parallel (\texttt{TP}) and sequence-parallel (\texttt{SP}) LLM layers execute a row-parallel GEMM followed immediately by an \texttt{AllReduce} or \texttt{ReduceScatter} and \texttt{RMSNorm}. Even with hardware NVSwitch Gen5 (\texttt{NVLS}) collectives on NVIDIA \texttt{GB200 NVL72}, unfused execution writes every GEMM partial tile to local HBM3e, executes a grid barrier, reads partials back from HBM3e for reduction, and exposes $15$--$45\,\mu\text{s}$ per collective across $96$ collectives per token.

We present a \textbf{Two-Regime Zero-HBM-Traffic Collective-GEMM Architecture} that streams finished Blackwell \texttt{tcgen05} Tensor Memory (\texttt{TMEM}) epilogue tiles directly onto the NVLink-5/IMEX fabric without touching local HBM3e. Through systematic microarchitectural probing across $4\times$, $8\times$, and $16\times$ \texttt{GB200} (up to $4$ physical NVL72 trays in one \texttt{IMEX} \texttt{ComputeDomain}), we establish three hardware laws:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Zero MMA Backpressure:} Issuing continuous NVSwitch \texttt{multimem.red} from epilogue warps concurrently with \texttt{tcgen05.mma} on the same SM incurs \textbf{$0.0\%$ ($-3.9\%$) MMA stall} ($67.2$ vs.\ $70.0\text{ cyc/MMA}$).
    \item \textbf{The SMEM-Coalescing Cliff:} Direct TMEM-register \texttt{multimem.red} (\texttt{lane=row}, stride $16\text{ KiB}$) suffers $\sim 32\times$ uncoalesced NVSwitch transaction amplification; staging through a $32\text{ KB}$ SMEM coalescing buffer accelerates bit-exact \texttt{SWAR} reductions by \textbf{$5.36\times$} and hides \textbf{$90.2\%$--$93.4\%$} of collective latency on 1-tray $4{\times}$\texttt{GB200}.
    \item \textbf{The Multi-Tray Multicast Write-Amplification Crossover:} Across $W \in \{8, 16\}$ GPUs, unsharded all-rank \texttt{multimem.red} triggers $W\times$ multicast write amplification above $8\text{ MiB}$, capping throughput at $\sim 85\text{ GB/s/GPU}$ ($1{,}698\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}4096$, $W{=}16$). Switching to \textbf{Tile-Sharded \texttt{Epi-P2P-RS}}---where epilogue warps stream tiles over $775\text{ GB/s}$ cross-tray IMEX NVLink-5 directly into \texttt{owner(tile)}'s inbox, followed by deterministic rank-order \texttt{FP32} local HBM3e summation ($5.7\text{ TB/s}$) and single-writer \texttt{multimem.st} broadcast---eliminates $W\times$ switch amplification and guarantees \textbf{$0 / 1{,}048{,}576$ cross-tray bit differences}.
\end{enumerate}
On live $4$-tray \textbf{$16{\times}$\texttt{GB200} IMEX} (\texttt{TP=16}, $[M,512]{\times}[512,8192]$), our Two-Regime engine beats \texttt{cuBLAS + NCCL16 + RMSNorm} at \emph{every} batch size $M \in [16, 4096]$ by \textbf{$1.40\times$--$10.72\times$} ($24.0$ vs.\ $257.1\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}16$; $267.6$ vs.\ $374.7\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}4096$, $64\text{ MiB}$), and beats \texttt{cuBLAS + NCCL16 ReduceScatter} at every $M$ by \textbf{$2.33\times$--$6.40\times$}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In large-scale \texttt{TP=8} and \texttt{TP=16} LLM serving and training across NVIDIA \texttt{GB200 NVL72} racks, row-parallel projections ($Y = \sum_{r=0}^{W-1} A_r B_r^\top$) followed by \texttt{RMSNorm} dominate inter-token latency at small batches ($M \le 256$) and consume substantial fabric and HBM3e bandwidth at large prefill/training batches ($M \in [1024, 4096]$). Conventional overlap schemes (chunked streams, \texttt{CUTLASS} split-GEMM + \texttt{NCCL}) still materialize GEMM outputs to local HBM3e and pay kernel-launch, barrier, and \texttt{NCCL} software-scheduling floors ($\approx 80$--$200\,\mu\text{s}$ across $8$--$16$ GPUs).

Blackwell's NVSwitch Gen5 (\texttt{NVLS}) exposes memory-mapped switch reductions (\texttt{multimem.red}) and single-writer broadcasts (\texttt{multimem.st}), while \texttt{IMEX} (\texttt{CU\_MEM\_HANDLE\_TYPE\_FABRIC}) maps remote trays' HBM3e directly into each SM's virtual address space at full $775\text{ GB/s}$ NVLink-5 peer bandwidth. We show how to fuse both primitives directly into the \texttt{tcgen05} \texttt{TMEM} epilogue---and why \emph{neither} primitive alone wins across all message sizes, leading to our Two-Regime Collective-GEMM architecture.

\section{Blackwell NVSwitch Gen5 Epilogue Physics}

\subsection{Law 1: Zero MMA Backpressure Under Concurrent \texttt{multimem.red}}
A central architectural risk of fusing network instructions into a Tensor-Core kernel is whether long-latency NVSwitch \texttt{multimem.red} stores backpressure SM load/store queues (LSU) or L2 ports and stall \texttt{tcgen05.mma} issue. On live $4{\times}$\texttt{GB200} ($152$ SMs/GPU), we ran a warp-specialized stress kernel where Warp~4 issued $4{,}096$ back-to-back \texttt{tcgen05.mma.cta\_group::1} instructions while Warps~0--3 continuously saturated either idle sleep, local HBM3e stores, NVSwitch \texttt{multimem.red.v4.bf16x2}, or bit-exact \texttt{multimem.red.u64} \texttt{SWAR}. Measured issue latency was $69.97\text{ cyc/MMA}$ (idle), $69.08\text{ cyc}$ (local HBM), \textbf{$67.18\text{ cyc}$ ($-3.98\%$, \texttt{multimem.bf16})}, and \textbf{$67.30\text{ cyc}$ ($-3.81\%$, \texttt{multimem.u64})}---proving \textbf{zero MMA issue stall}.

\subsection{Law 2: The $32\times$ Uncoalesced \texttt{multimem.red} Amplification Cliff}
Blackwell's \texttt{tcgen05.ld.32x32b.x32} maps thread \texttt{tid} to output \emph{row} \texttt{tid}, placing adjacent warp lanes $N \times 2\text{ B} = 16\text{ KiB}$ apart in row-major output tensors. Issuing \texttt{multimem.red} directly from \texttt{TMEM} registers bypasses L1 coalescing and fires $32$ uncoalesced NVSwitch transactions per warp, slowing $4{\times}$\texttt{GB200} \texttt{Epi-NVLS} to $0.70\times$--$0.87\times$ of unfused \texttt{GEMM+NVLS} (and slowing \texttt{SWAR u64} to $5{,}151\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}4096$).

Staging each $128{\times}128$ \texttt{BF16} tile through $32\text{ KB}$ of shared memory and having $16$ contiguous threads write contiguous $256\text{ B}$ row bursts ($100\%$ warp-coalesced) immediately flips every shape $M \in [16, 4096]$ on $4{\times}$\texttt{GB200} to \textbf{$1.12\times$--$1.51\times$ faster than unfused \texttt{GEMM+NVLS}}, hiding \textbf{$90.2\%$} ($137.5 \to 13.5\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}1024$) to \textbf{$93.4\%$} ($413.0 \to 27.3\,\mu\text{s}$ at $M{=}4096$) of exposed collective latency and accelerating bit-exact \texttt{SWAR} by \textbf{$5.36\times$} ($5{,}152 \to 962\,\mu\text{s}$).

\subsection{Law 3: The Multi-Tray Multicast Write-Amplification Crossover}
On $W \in \{8, 16\}$ GPUs across $2$--$4$ physical trays, unsharded \texttt{multimem.red} pushes every GPU's full $S = 2MN$-byte output into the switch, which must multicast the reduced result back to all $W$ GPUs ($W \times S$ total switch egress, capping per-GPU effective bandwidth at $\sim B_{\text{NVL}} / W \approx 85\text{ GB/s}$ at $W{=}8$ and $\sim 45\text{ GB/s}$ at $W{=}16$). By contrast, sharded \texttt{ReduceScatter + AllGather} sends $(W{-}1)/W \cdot S$ bytes P2P and broadcasts only $S/W$ per rank via \texttt{multimem.st}, sustaining near full $775\text{ GB/s}$ bisection bandwidth regardless of $W$.

\section{Two-Regime Architecture: \texttt{Epi-NVLS} + \texttt{Epi-P2P-RS}}

Our 1D-TMA double-buffered \texttt{tcgen05} GEMM switches cleanly between two zero-HBM-partial-write epilogues:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regime 1 (\texttt{Epi-NVLS-TilePipe}, $M \le 32$):} Coalesced SMEM $\to$ \texttt{multimem.red.v4.bf16x2} directly into the \texttt{IMEX} multicast VA, incrementing a per-row-strip arrival counter \texttt{mc\_row\_flag[tm]}. A concurrent stream launches \texttt{RMSNorm} immediately, polling \texttt{uc\_row\_flag[tm]} and normalizing each $128$-row strip as it arrives.
    \item \textbf{Regime 2 (\texttt{Epi-P2P-RS} \& \texttt{Epi-P2P-RS+MC}, $M \ge 64$):} Column tiles are partitioned evenly across all $W$ ranks (\texttt{owner = tn / (tiles\_n / W)}, $N_{\text{shard}} = N / W$). Staggering tile traversal order by \texttt{my\_rank} eliminates incast. Epilogue warps issue coalesced \texttt{st.relaxed.sys.global.v4.u32} directly across $775\text{ GB/s}$ \texttt{IMEX FABRIC} peer mappings into \texttt{peer\_va[owner]} at rank-private slot \texttt{my\_rank} (zero local HBM partial write, zero atomic contention!). After a $4\,\mu\text{s}$ \texttt{NVLS} barrier, each owner sums its $W$ local-HBM3e partials at $5.7\text{ TB/s}$ in \textbf{canonical rank order $r = 0 \dots W{-}1$ in \texttt{FP32}}---producing either (a) the exact Megatron-SP \texttt{ReduceScatter} shard (\texttt{Epi-P2P-RS}) or (b) a single-writer \texttt{multimem.st.v4.f32} broadcast to all $W$ GPUs (\texttt{Epi-P2P-RS+MC}) with \textbf{$0$ cross-rank bit differences}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Fusing \texttt{tcgen05} \texttt{TMEM} epilogues directly onto NVLink-5/NVSwitch Gen5 via our Two-Regime \texttt{Epi-NVLS} + \texttt{Epi-P2P-RS} architecture eliminates collective HBM3e writes, overcomes multi-tray NVSwitch write amplification, guarantees bit-exactness across $16$ GPUs / $4$ physical trays, and outperforms \texttt{cuBLAS + NCCL16} by $1.40\times$--$10.72\times$ (\texttt{AllReduce}) and $2.33\times$--$6.40\times$ (\texttt{ReduceScatter}) across the entire batch spectrum.
